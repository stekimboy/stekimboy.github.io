Rotating detonation engine CFD study

CFD design and simulation study, October 2025 to February 2026

A CFD study of a small-scale hydrogen–air rotating detonation engine. An RDE replaces the steady flame of a conventional combustor with a detonation wave that travels continuously around an annular channel.

I started with a literature review covering F. A. Bykovskii, P. Wolański, Purdue's solid-fuel designs and the GE–Lockheed ramjet work. I then built the injector, chamber and annulus geometry from published papers and modeled the engine in CONVERGE CFD, where it sustained a rotating detonation wave. I analyzed the pressure, temperature and species fields in Tecplot to track the wave and verify that it kept propagating.

GeometryInjector, chamber and annulus from published papers
SolverCONVERGE CFD
Post-processingTecplot

Tools: CONVERGE CFD, Tecplot.

AeroForge

Independent project, July to August 2026

A design tool for small RC aircraft. You give it an aircraft type, a cruise speed and a size limit, and it suggests several wing designs, shows them in 3D, and exports CAD files ready to 3D print.

I checked a few of the generated designs with CFD on SimScale; the small figures show airflow and surface pressure over one flying wing. One of the generated wings was printed in LW-PLA and test-flown.

Tools: Python, SimScale, Fusion 360.

Repository on GitHub

TC001 thermal goggles

Independent project

Head-mounted thermal vision built from a Raspberry Pi 4, a TOPDON TC001 thermal camera and a pair of analog FPV goggles. The TC001 is made to plug into a phone; I wanted it to work as a headset you just power on and wear.

The Pi reads the camera's video, cleans up the image so it's easy to see, and sends it to the goggles over the composite video output. It boots straight into the thermal view.

Rook 2020 MK2 CoreXY printer

Independent project, November 2024 to May 2025

A scratch-built CoreXY 3D printer based on Rolohaun Design's open-source Rook 2020 MK2. I sourced the parts list and assembled the motion system, taking it from bare aluminum extrusions to a first print.

I wired in an SKR Pico control board, a Raspberry Pi, thermistors, endstops and cooling. I then set up sensorless homing, tuned input shaping, and set up a VPN so I could monitor prints remotely.

ControlSKR Pico with a Raspberry Pi

Tools: SKR Pico, Raspberry Pi, PrusaSlicer.
